Two traditions, one invite

A Kerala Muhurtham and a Maharashtrian wedding back to back in Mumbai, then a reception at home in Pookkottukavu a week later. The suite leans on what both families know: the golden Muthukkuda parasol from Kerala temple processions, hand-drawn banana leaves at the edges, and a cursive NV monogram in deep green. Honouring sixty years

The invitations I made with my family for my father's sixtieth birthday and my parents' vow renewal at the Tennis Pavilion, Bangalore Palace. Marigold torans, bronze temple bells and green ringneck parrots, the motifs we grew up with, set in classic serif type with a hand-lettered gold sixty on warm textured paper. Hello, I'm Anadhya

I'm a graphic designer with a background in product design and a love for all things visual. Most days I'm at Kiana Bespoke Invitations in Bengaluru, drawing invitations and stationery suites for weddings.

I'm drawn to the little details: the beaded tassels on a parasol, the exact pink of a lotus, which way a monogram's tail should curl. Almost every project I take on starts in a sketchbook.

Before paper, I studied industrial and product design at Raffles Design Institute, which is why I still think about how a card feels in the hand and how a suite unfolds when it's opened.

My working rule is take one thing away, then look again. Whether it's a family's biggest day or a bar's whole crew of characters, I want the work to feel intentional, personal and a little unexpected.
